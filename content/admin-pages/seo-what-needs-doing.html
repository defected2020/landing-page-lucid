The one real problem

Searching for "Lucid Code Labs" across Reddit, LinkedIn, YouTube and Clutch returns nothing for this company. The search engine's own summary said it could not find a company by that name. What it returns instead is a crowded namespace, and every occupant is better documented than we are.

WhoWhat they have that we do not

Lucid Reality LabsClutch 100 ranking, VR/AR trade press, active LinkedIn

The first row is the painful one: a registered Berlin AI agency, our city and our sector, with company-registry records a machine can verify.

We added founder: [Aline, George] to the site's structured data and re-ran the search. It still returns Lucid Labs' founder by name and still reports no founder information for Lucid Code Labs. Schema on our own domain is a claim, not corroboration. A third party has to say it too.

This is why AI search matters differently from Google ranking: an assistant resolves an entity before it cites a page. There is currently no entity to resolve to, and a better-evidenced near-namesake to resolve to instead. No amount of on-page work fixes that. Note also that Berlin does not disambiguate us — Lucid Labs is also Berlin. Consistently writing the name in full, never "Lucid Labs", is the cheapest thing that does.

Waiting on a decision

Three code changes are drafted and blocked only on a judgement that is not mine to make.

1. The word "trained"

data/portfolioProjects.js:113 and data/caseStudyContent.js:142,147 all say Lucid "trained 9 distinct AI companions". If those companions were prompt-designed rather than fine-tuned, "trained" overstates the capability.

This matters more now than it did. That content carries George's name through Person schema, and trust is the highest-weighted factor in the E-E-A-T scorecard. One word, three places, thirty seconds once the accurate verb is known.

2. The service-page subtitles

Five service pages open with "Transform your …". The content scorer flags it, and the blog contains nothing like it. These were deliberately left alone because several double as description= meta and createServiceSchema text, so rewriting them changes what Google has already indexed. Worth doing as its own considered pass.

3. Two thin case studies

/work/awakenest and /work/myth-os are thin because no repository existed to verify anything against. Any real detail added to data/caseStudyContent.js helps; nothing can be invented.

What not to do

  • Do not add /llms.txt. Google states plainly that it neither helps nor hurts. If anyone sells it as an AI ranking lever, that tells you what the rest of their advice is worth.
  • Do not buy links or "SEO packages". Purchased backlinks and bulk directory spam are what Google's spam updates exist to catch.
  • Do not trust "guaranteed page 1". Any vendor leading with a guarantee has disqualified themselves. Google says so itself.
  • Do not mass-produce AI content. Thin generated posts get demoted site-wide. Everything currently published was written to a bar where 141 unsupported claims were removed before launch — hold new content to it.
  • Do not backdate posts. datePublished feeds structured data under a named author, so an invented date is a false claim — and recent content is substantially more likely to be cited in AI answers, so an older date costs visibility rather than buying it.
  • Do not rewrite content "for AI". Google's own guidance rejects AI-specific rephrasing, chunking and keyword rewriting as ineffective.
